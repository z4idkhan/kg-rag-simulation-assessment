The simulation incorporates several realistic elements, such as the use of Q-learning to model student agents' adaptations to rewards and penalties, social peer influence, and the distinction between direct copying and AI-assisted plagiarism. **B. Top 3 most important realism gaps:**

1. **Simplification of academic integrity policies**: The simulation only considers two intervention conditions, which might not accurately reflect the nuances of real-world academic integrity policies. In reality, institutions often implement a range of measures, including education, prevention, and punishment, which can vary in their effectiveness and enforcement.
2. **Lack of contextual factors**: The simulation does not account for contextual factors that can influence plagiarism behavior, such as the type of assignments, the level of student engagement, or the instructor's teaching style. To enhance the realism of the simulation, I recommend introducing **variable intervention strategies**. Instead of the binary no-intervention vs. active-intervention conditions, the simulation could incorporate a range of intervention strategies, such as:

* Different types of educational programs (e.g., workshops, online modules)
* Varied levels of punishment (e.g., warning, grade penalty, failure)
* Different detection methods (e.g., manual review, automated tools)
* Peer-led initiatives (e.g., student ambassadors, peer review)

By introducing variable intervention strategies, the simulation can better capture the complexities of real-world academic integrity policies and provide more nuanced insights into the effectiveness of different approaches.
