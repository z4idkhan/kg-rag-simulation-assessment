A. OVERALL REALISM
PARTLY
The simulation explicitly models student decision-making under probabilistic detection and compares intervention strategies, which reflects core aspects of academic integrity dynamics. However, it simplifies detection probabilities and omits key contextual factors, limiting its ability to fully represent real-world academic environments.

B. TOP 3 REALISM GAPS
1. Detection probabilities are fixed and uniform across students, whereas real academic integrity systems often feature variable detection rates based on instructor vigilance, assignment type, or student risk profiles.
2. The model does not specify how AI-assisted plagiarism is technically distinguished from direct copying or honest submission, leaving unclear whether agents have differential capabilities or knowledge of detection thresholds.
3. Peer network structure and influence mechanisms are mentioned but not detailed; it is unspecified whether interactions follow homophily, authority gradients, or random tie formation, which significantly affects how interventions propagate through the student population.

C. ONE MINIMUM REVISION
Add a parameter defining the proportion of assignments where AI-assisted plagiarism is technically distinguishable from direct copying. Evaluate whether varying this proportion (e.g., 0%, 50%, 100%) changes the prevalence of dishonest behavior under the intervention condition, providing insight into how detection specificity affects policy efficacy.
