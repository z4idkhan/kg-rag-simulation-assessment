A. OVERALL REALISM
PARTLY
The simulation captures a core mechanism of academic integrity by modeling the trade-off between risk (detection probability) and reward using Q-learning, which aligns with the definition of reinforcement learning as an agent learning behavior through rewards and penalties. However, the realism is limited by the binary nature of the detection probabilities and the lack of specified environmental dynamics beyond simple peer interaction, which may oversimplify the complex social and institutional factors involved in plagiarism.

B. TOP 3 REALISM GAPS
1. **Gap:** The detection probabilities are fixed constants (0.55 for direct copying, 0.25 for AI-assisted) rather than dynamic variables.
   **Why it matters:** In real academic environments, detection efficacy can change over time due to improved detection tools or evolving institutional policies; static probabilities may not reflect the adaptive nature of both plagiarists and detectors.
   **Status:** Hypothesis needing more information. The description states the probabilities are 0.55 and 0.25 but does not specify if they are static throughout the 16 weeks or if they evolve.

2. **Gap:** The "active-intervention" condition is not defined in terms of specific mechanisms or parameters.
   **Why it matters:** Without specifying what the intervention entails (e.g., increased detection, educational modules, peer pressure), it is impossible to assess whether the finding that AI-assisted plagiarism remains prevalent is due to the nature of the intervention or a flaw in the model's response to it.
   **Status:** Directly supported by the description's omission. The text mentions comparing a "no-intervention" with an "active-intervention" condition but provides no details on the latter's implementation.

3. **Gap:** The impact of the local peer network on behavior is unspecified.
   **Why it matters:** Academic integrity is heavily influenced by social norms and peer influence; without specifying how the network structure or peer actions affect the Q-learning rewards/penalties, it is unclear if the model captures the social contagion aspect of dishonesty.
   **Status:** Hypothesis needing more information. The description states students interact through local peer networks but does not explain how these interactions modify the learning process.

C. ONE MINIMUM REVISION
Define the specific mechanism of the "active-intervention" condition (e.g., a 20% increase in detection probability for all plagiarism types) and report the resulting change in total dishonest behavior compared to the no-intervention condition. This allows for a direct evaluation of whether the intervention effectively reduces the specific behavior of interest, providing a clearer causal link between the intervention and the outcome.
