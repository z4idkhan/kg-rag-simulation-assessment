B. TOP 3 REALISM GAPS
1. **Gap:** The simulation uses fixed detection probabilities (0.55 for direct copying, 0.25 for AI-assisted) without specifying how these values are derived or if they change over time.
   **Why it matters:** In reality, detection efficacy varies by institution, course level, and the sophistication of the plagiarism. Static probabilities may not reflect the dynamic arms race between detection tools and evasion techniques, potentially skewing the long-term prevalence of AI-assisted plagiarism.
   **Status:** Hypothesis needing more information. The description states the values are fixed but does not provide empirical grounding for them.

2. **Gap:** The "local peer networks" are mentioned as an interaction mechanism, but the specific structure (e.g., scale-free, random, clustered) and the weight of peer influence on the reward function are unspecified.
   **Why it matters:** Academic dishonesty is heavily influenced by social norms and peer behavior. Without defining the network topology and the magnitude of social reinforcement, it is difficult to assess whether the observed increase in AI-assisted plagiarism is driven by realistic social contagion or an artifact of the model's specific network assumptions.
   **Status:** Hypothesis needing more information. The description confirms interaction exists but omits critical structural details.

3. **Gap:** The model treats "AI-assisted plagiarism" as a single, homogeneous action with a specific penalty/reward profile, without distinguishing between varying degrees of AI use (e.g., minor editing vs. full generation).
   **Why it matters:** In practice, AI use exists on a spectrum. Lumping all AI-assisted behaviors into one category with a single detection probability (0.25) may oversimplify the risk-reward calculation for agents, potentially misrepresenting why this behavior remains prevalent under intervention.
   **Status:** Directly supported by the description. The text explicitly lists "AI-assisted plagiarism" as one of three discrete choices, implying a binary or categorical treatment rather than a gradient.

C. ONE MINIMUM REVISION
**Recommendation:** Introduce heterogeneity in the detection probability for AI-assisted plagiarism by modeling it as a function of the "level" of AI use (e.g., low, medium, high) rather than a single fixed value.
**Evaluation:** Compare the total volume of dishonest behavior and the specific distribution of AI-use levels between the original simulation and the revised one. If the revised model shows a more nuanced response to interventions (e.g., low-level AI use persists while high-level drops), it suggests the original binary assumption was oversimplifying the behavioral dynamics.
