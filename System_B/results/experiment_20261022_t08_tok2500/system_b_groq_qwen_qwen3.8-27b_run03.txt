2. **Static and Simplified Detection Probabilities**: The model assigns fixed detection probabilities (0.55 for direct copying, 0.25 for AI-assisted). This matters because real-world detection is dynamic, dependent on the sophistication of the plagiarism type, the robustness of institutional tools, and the volume of submissions. Treating detection as a constant binary likelihood underestimates the adaptive nature of both cheaters and detectors. This is a hypothesis needing more information, as the description specifies the values but does not justify them based on empirical data or discuss their variability.
3. **Lack of Heterogeneity in Student Agents**: The model uses 30 "student agents" without specifying differences in academic ability, prior knowledge of integrity policies, or risk tolerance. This matters because academic dishonesty is often correlated with specific student subgroups; a homogeneous agent pool may produce average-out effects that mask realistic polarization in behavior. This is a hypothesis needing more information, as the description confirms the use of agents and peer networks but does not specify the distribution of agent traits.

**Evaluation**: To evaluate if this improves the simulation, conduct a sensitivity analysis by varying the penalty for detection (e.g., from minor grade deduction to course failure) and observing if the prevalence of AI-assisted plagiarism changes predictably.
