### A. Overall Realism Judgment

Partly. The simulation incorporates several realistic elements, such as the use of Q-learning to model how students adapt their behavior based on rewards and penalties, and the consideration of social peer influence. However, the simplification of plagiarism detection probabilities to fixed values (0.55 for direct copying and 0.25 for AI-assisted plagiarism) and the binary approach to intervention (no intervention vs. active intervention) may not fully capture the complexities of real-world plagiarism detection and prevention strategies.

### B. Top 3 Most Important Realism Gaps

1. **Simplification of Detection Probabilities**: The fixed detection probabilities for direct copying and AI-assisted plagiarism oversimplify the real-world challenges in detecting plagiarism, especially with evolving AI technologies. In reality, detection probabilities can vary widely based on the sophistication of the plagiarism, the effectiveness of detection tools, and the diligence of instructors.

2. **Lack of Granularity in Intervention Strategies**: The simulation only considers two intervention conditions (no intervention and active intervention), which does not reflect the variety of strategies that can be employed in educational settings, such as educational campaigns, stringent penalty policies, or the use of plagiarism detection tools as a deterrent.

3. **Limited Agent Behavior Complexity**: While the simulation allows agents to choose between honest submission, direct copying, and AI-assisted plagiarism, it may not account for other factors that influence student behavior, such as personal values, perceived consequences, the pressure to achieve high grades, or the genuine lack of understanding of what constitutes plagiarism. Incorporating these factors could make the simulation more realistic.

### C. One Minimum Recommended Revision

**Dynamic Detection Probabilities and Intervention Strategies**: Implement a more dynamic system where detection probabilities and the effectiveness of intervention strategies can change over time. This could be based on the agents' actions (e.g., if many agents start using AI-assisted plagiarism, the detection probability for this method could increase as educators become more aware and adapt their detection methods) or on external factors (e.g., updates in plagiarism detection software, changes in institutional policies). This would make the simulation more reflective of real-world scenarios, where both plagiarism methods and detection techniques evolve over time. Additionally, allowing for a range of intervention strategies and evaluating their impacts could provide more nuanced insights into effective plagiarism prevention.
