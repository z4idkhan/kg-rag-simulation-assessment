B. TOP 3 REALISM GAPS
1. **Binary behavioral choices**: The model limits student actions to honest submission, direct copying, or AI-assisted plagiarism. This simplification may not reflect the full spectrum of academic misconduct (e.g., paraphrasing without citation, contract cheating) and risks misrepresenting how students navigate ambiguous integrity scenarios. *Hypothesis needing more information.*
2. **Static detection probabilities**: The fixed 0.55 and 0.25 probabilities for direct and AI-assisted plagiarism ignore the likelihood that detection rates change as students adapt their strategies or as the intervention takes effect. In reality, detection efficacy is often dynamic and responsive to behavioral shifts. *Hypothesis needing more information.*
3. **Absence of penalty severity and nature**: The description does not specify the consequences of detection (e.g., grade penalties, disciplinary action, social stigma). Without defined penalties, the Q-learning reward structure is ambiguous, making it unclear how agents are expected to balance risk and reward. *Directly supported by the description.*

C. ONE MINIMUM REVISION
Add explicit, non-zero penalty values for detected dishonesty (e.g., grade reduction or course failure) and clarify how these penalties are applied differently for direct versus AI-assisted plagiarism. Evaluate the improvement by observing whether the intervention condition produces a measurable shift in the proportion of AI-assisted plagiarism over time compared to the no-intervention condition, indicating that agents are responding to the cost of detection.
