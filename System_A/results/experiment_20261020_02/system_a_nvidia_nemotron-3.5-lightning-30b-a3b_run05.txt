B. TOP 3 REALISM GAPS

1.  **Absence of Student Agency and Motivation**: The description states agents update behavior based on rewards and penalties via Q-learning, but it does not specify the reward structure. Without defined incentives (e.g., grade pressure, fear of failure), the model cannot realistically simulate why students choose dishonest paths, reducing its ability to capture the motivational drivers of plagiarism.
2.  **Lack of Explicit Network Dynamics**: While students interact through "local peer networks," the description provides no details on network topology, tie strength, or how influence propagates. It is unclear if peers model behavior, enforce norms, or if isolation affects risk perception, making the "peer network" feature vague and its impact on dishonesty unsupported.
3.  **Static Detection Probabilities**: The model fixes detection probabilities at 0.55 for direct copying and 0.25 for AI-assisted plagiarism. In reality, detection efficacy often changes as students adapt tactics or as interventions are refined. Fixing these values prevents the simulation from capturing the dynamic interplay between evasion strategies and enforcement that characterizes actual academic integrity efforts.
